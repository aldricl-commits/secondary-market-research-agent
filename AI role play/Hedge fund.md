Analyze Stock EPS Narrative
Objective
Produce a hedge-fund-style, decision-useful view of a stock over the next 12 months. Determine:
	1. which operating variables can change earnings;
	2. whether those variables are durable and already visible in estimates;
	3. whether the narrative is improving or deteriorating, and how long it can persist;
	4. what the price already discounts;
	5. which catalyst or falsifier will resolve the disagreement.
Treat the stock outcome as:
earnings revision + valuation change driven by narrative/rates/risk + distributions ± path dependency
Do not reduce the conclusion to “good company” or “bad company.” A correct fundamental view can still be a bad trade when it is fully priced, crowded, late, or lacks a catalyst.
Select the task mode
Infer one mode from the request:
	• Full setup: Run the complete 12-month workflow.
	• Pre-earnings: Identify the market’s exam, whisper hurdle, asymmetry, and likely reaction function.
	• Post-earnings: Reconcile reported results, guidance, estimate changes, and price reaction.
	• Thesis update: Preserve the prior ledger when available and show only what changed.
	• Comparison or screen: Use identical definitions, cutoff dates, EPS bases, and valuation conventions.
	• Monitoring design: Build leading indicators, evidence cadence, thresholds, catalysts, and falsifiers.
Resolve ambiguous securities before making company-specific claims. State the ticker, listing, currency, price date, analysis cutoff, and forecast window.
Apply the research protocol
Use current information because prices, consensus, guidance, and narratives change quickly.
	1. Prioritize primary sources: filings, investor-relations releases, earnings decks, prepared remarks, call transcripts, official operating data, and regulator or industry statistics.
	2. Use secondary sources for consensus, channel checks, positioning, and context. Label their limitations.
	3. Distinguish reported fact, management claim, consensus, market whisper, and analyst inference.
	4. Cite every material current claim near the claim. Use exact dates, units, and currencies.
	5. Separate GAAP/IFRS EPS, company-adjusted EPS, and analyst-normalized core EPS. Reconcile share count, tax, interest, FX, one-offs, and stock compensation when material.
	6. Never invent consensus or whisper data. If unavailable, mark it unknown and infer the hurdle cautiously from guidance, prior revisions, valuation, and price reaction.
	7. Treat missing disclosure as unknown, not favorable evidence. Prefer ranges to false precision.
	8. Use at least the last 3–5 earnings periods for a full setup unless the business has changed so much that older periods are no longer comparable.
Read references/framework.md for scoring definitions, causal bridges, sector adaptations, and the setup matrix. Read references/output-template.md before producing the final memo.
Run the workflow
1. Frame the current market exam
Write one sentence:
	Over the next 12 months, the stock must prove ___ while avoiding ___; current pricing appears to assume ___.
Explain what changed the exam: earnings, guidance, macro conditions, competition, regulation, capital allocation, positioning, or valuation. Separate the company’s long-run quality from the stock’s current question.
2. Establish the expectations baseline
Record, when available:
	• reported results versus consensus and guidance;
	• current and 30/90/180-day forward EPS estimates and revision breadth;
	• management guidance, sell-side consensus, and market whisper;
	• forward valuation on a consistent EPS or cash-flow basis;
	• relative and absolute share-price performance;
	• sentiment or crowding proxies;
	• the good-news and bad-news hurdle implied by recent reactions.
Do not call something “priced in” merely because it is well known. Show evidence through valuation, estimates, positioning, or the stock’s reaction function.
3. Build the core-variable ledger
Select 3–7 variables that explain most of the next 12 months’ earnings variance. Avoid exhaustive company history.
For each variable, record:
	• what management and reported data said in each of the last 3–5 earnings periods;
	• current direction, acceleration, and surprise versus expectations;
	• causal link to revenue, margin, below-the-line items, and diluted EPS;
	• durability grade and expected half-life;
	• leading, coincident, or lagging status;
	• evidence state: verified, supported, open/testable, unfalsifiable, or disproved;
	• next observation date and explicit confirm/weakens/falsifies thresholds.
Distinguish a level effect from a growth-rate effect. A still-positive variable can be bearish if it is decelerating faster than estimates assume.
4. Construct the EPS revision bridge
Translate variables into a causal bridge:
volume × price/mix/take rate → revenue → gross profit → operating profit → pretax profit → net income → diluted EPS
For every material driver, estimate:
	• revision direction and probability;
	• magnitude range;
	• timing within 0–3, 3–6, or 6–12 months;
	• persistence beyond the forecast window;
	• quality: recurring versus one-off, operating versus financial/accounting, and cash versus non-cash;
	• whether consensus has incorporated it.
Assign an EPS state: U2, U1, N, D1, or D2. Do not average independent positives and negatives blindly; identify the variable that dominates the distribution and show residual tail risks.
5. Diagnose the narrative
Score the narrative from +2 to -2 and separately state:
	• momentum: improving, stable, or worsening;
	• half-life: event-driven, medium-cycle, or structural;
	• owner of the current burden of proof;
	• evidence needed to confirm or break it;
	• whether it is financially confirmed, financially contradicted, or still multiple-only.
Treat narrative as the market’s compressed causal belief, not news sentiment. A negative narrative with flat EPS can still compress the multiple; a positive narrative without earnings support is fragile.
6. Overlay pricing and positioning
Separate:
	• business truth: what is happening operationally;
	• estimate truth: what consensus will likely revise;
	• pricing truth: what the current valuation and reaction function already assume.
Reverse-engineer the current price into required growth, margin, cash flow, or EPS outcomes. Compare that hurdle with the variable ledger. Include crowding, liquidity, event gaps, and borrow constraints when relevant.
Answer:
	1. What must happen for the stock to rise despite no EPS upgrade?
	2. What must happen for the stock to fall despite no EPS downgrade?
	3. Which plausible outcome would surprise the market most?
7. Map the catalyst path
Create a dated 3/6/12-month sequence. For every catalyst include:
	• observable metric or event;
	• consensus or implied hurdle;
	• expected EPS effect;
	• expected narrative effect;
	• likely market sensitivity;
	• confirm, weaken, and falsify thresholds.
Reject undated catalysts such as “eventually recognized.” If the thesis is right but the evidence arrives after the investment horizon, classify the timing mismatch explicitly.
8. Build scenarios and classify the setup
Create bull, base, and bear cases with probabilities summing to 100%. Use EPS revision ranges, narrative states, valuation regimes, and catalysts rather than unsupported price targets.
Classify the stock as one of:
	• Clean Long
	• Contrarian Long
	• Narrative-Only / Tactical
	• Wait / Random-Walk Candidate
	• Avoid
	• Tactical Short
	• Structural Short
	• Correct View, Bad Trade / No Trade
Apply the setup matrix in the framework reference. Modify the classification for valuation, crowding, catalyst timing, and downside asymmetry. Never infer the user’s personal risk capacity.
9. Define the update rule
End with:
	• the single most important leading indicator;
	• the next two evidence dates;
	• the threshold for moving one EPS grade up or down;
	• the condition that changes the narrative score;
	• the explicit thesis breaker;
	• what is still unknowable.
For an update, show prior view → new evidence → revised view. Do not rewrite history after the fact.
Quality gate
Before finalizing, verify that the analysis:
	• leads with a dated verdict rather than company background;
	• identifies only the variables that can change 12-month EPS or the multiple;
	• separates variable direction, durability, and estimate incorporation;
	• distinguishes positive level from positive acceleration;
	• distinguishes EPS direction from narrative direction;
	• separates consensus from whisper and unknown data;
	• explains what is priced in with evidence;
	• includes the strongest opposing case;
	• includes catalyst timing and falsification thresholds;
	• uses calibrated probabilities and exposes uncertainty;
	• avoids automatic conclusions such as “EPS up means buy” or “EPS down means short.”
