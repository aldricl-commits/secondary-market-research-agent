Analyze Stock Price and Volume Structure
Objective
Produce a decision-useful technical view of a stock from price action and volume alone. Determine:
	1. the trend state on weekly and daily timeframes;
	2. the key levels that matter and why;
	3. whether volume confirms or contradicts the price structure;
	4. relative strength versus sector and index;
	5. a setup classification with entry, stop, invalidation, and risk-reward.
Treat technical analysis as a timing and risk-management overlay, not prophecy. The chart shows what participants have done, not what the business is worth.
Do not use fundamentals. Earnings quality, valuation, guidance, and narrative belong to other analysts. Reference a fundamental event only as a dated risk (an earnings date is a volatility event, not a thesis input). If asked for a fundamental opinion, decline and return the technical view.
Evidence rules
	• Use split- and dividend-adjusted prices. State the adjustment basis if ambiguous.
	• State the data cutoff, exchange, and currency at the top of the analysis.
	• Use at least 2 years of daily data and 5 years of weekly data when available; state the actual window used.
	• Never analyze a chart pattern without its volume context. A pattern without volume is a shape, not evidence.
	• Distinguish observed price fact ("price closed above the 200-day on 3x average volume") from pattern interpretation ("this resembles a Stage 2 breakout"). Label which is which.
	• If data for an indicator is unavailable, mark it unknown. Do not improvise values or infer them from memory.
	• Prefer closing prices for level definitions; note when an intraday extreme is the relevant reference.
Workflow
1. Set up the analysis
Record:
	• ticker, exchange, currency, data cutoff, timeframes used;
	• weekly chart for trend regime, daily chart for execution;
	• liquidity: average daily dollar volume and typical spread; flag anything too illiquid for the implied position size;
	• upcoming binary events, especially the next earnings date. A binary event inside the trade horizon overrides technical signals: either size for the gap or wait until it clears.
2. Map the trend structure
	• Mark the sequence of swing highs and swing lows on both timeframes. Higher highs and higher lows define an uptrend; state where the sequence last broke.
	• Record the 50-day and 200-day moving averages: slope of each, price position relative to each, and any recent crossover with its date.
	• Classify the Weinstein stage: Stage 1 base, Stage 2 advance, Stage 3 top, Stage 4 decline. State the evidence for the stage call.
	• Use ADX only if trend strength is genuinely ambiguous; otherwise omit it.
	• When timeframes conflict, the weekly sets the regime and the daily sets the tactic. A daily buy signal inside a weekly Stage 4 is a counter-trend trade and must be labeled as one.
3. Read momentum
	• RSI(14): identify the operating regime (bull regimes hold 40–90, bear regimes hold 10–60), not just overbought/oversold levels.
	• MACD: direction and position relative to zero on both timeframes.
	• Rate of change: is the advance or decline accelerating or decelerating?
	• Count a divergence only when price structure confirms it — a bearish divergence matters after a lower high forms, not before. An unconfirmed divergence is a watch item, not a signal.
4. Analyze volume and participation
This is the core of the analysis. Price tells you what happened; volume tells you who meant it.
	• Compare the volume trend with the price trend: rising price on shrinking volume is a weakening advance; falling price on shrinking volume is a weakening decline.
	• Compute or estimate the up-day/down-day volume ratio over the recent swing.
	• State the direction of OBV or accumulation/distribution and whether it confirms or diverges from price.
	• Check volume on breakouts versus pullbacks. A breakout without volume expansion is suspect; a pullback on light volume after a high-volume advance is constructive. Treat a volume-less breakout as failure-prone until proven otherwise.
	• Look for climax volume: capitulation spikes at lows, blow-off spikes at highs, and churning (huge volume, no progress) near tops.
	• Apply Wyckoff phases as an interpretive frame, labeled as interpretation: accumulation versus distribution range, spring or upthrust, sign of strength or weakness, test quality. State which phase reading the volume supports and what would disprove it.
5. Define the key levels
Identify and tag every level with why it matters and what invalidates it:
	• prior swing highs and lows on both timeframes;
	• gap zones: breakaway, continuation, exhaustion; unfilled gaps as magnets or support;
	• anchored VWAP from major events — IPO, the last earnings gap, the major cycle low — as the average holder's cost basis;
	• volume-by-price shelves: high-volume nodes act as support and resistance, low-volume pockets let price move fast;
	• round numbers when order flow visibly respects them.
Reject any level you cannot justify. "It looks like support" is not a reason; "three daily closes defended it on above-average volume" is.
6. Measure relative strength
	• Compute the RS line versus the sector ETF and versus the index over 3, 6, and 12 months. State the trend of each.
	• Check new-high/new-low behavior: is the stock making new highs before or after the index?
	• Flag RS divergence at index turning points — a stock holding its lows while the index breaks down is relative accumulation; a stock lagging a rising index is relative distribution.
	• A breakout with deteriorating RS is a lower-quality breakout. Say so.
7. Overlay positioning and sentiment (optional)
Include only when data is available, and label availability explicitly:
	• short interest and days-to-cover;
	• put/call skew;
	• unusual options activity.
Treat these as contrarian context, not signals. High short interest explains squeeze potential; it does not create a buy signal. If unavailable, write "positioning data unknown" and move on.
8. Synthesize and classify the setup
Classify the stock as exactly one of:
	• Confirmed Uptrend
	• Pullback-in-Uptrend (buyable dip)
	• Base Building (watch)
	• Distribution Top (reduce)
	• Confirmed Downtrend (avoid or short)
	• Bottoming Attempt (speculative)
	• No Edge
For any actionable classification, specify:
	• entry zone with the trigger condition;
	• stop: the invalidation level and the structural reason it invalidates the setup, not an arbitrary percentage;
	• first target with the level-based reason;
	• risk-reward ratio; reject and reclassify to watch any setup below 2:1;
	• position-sizing note scaled to volatility: state the ATR(14) and size so the stop distance is a survivable loss.
Do not force a trade. "No Edge" is a complete and acceptable answer.
9. Define the update rule
End with:
	• the specific price/volume event that flips the classification up or down;
	• the level whose loss or reclaim triggers reassessment;
	• the next earnings date as a forced review point regardless of price action;
	• what the chart currently cannot tell you.
For an update, show prior classification → new price/volume evidence → revised classification. Do not redraw levels after the fact to fit the outcome.
Quality gate
Before finalizing, verify that the analysis:
	• leads with the setup classification, not chart narration;
	• contains no fundamental claims — no valuation, earnings, or narrative arguments;
	• tags every level with a reason and an invalidation;
	• pairs every signal with the condition that negates it;
	• avoids indicator soup: price and volume are primary; volume tools (OBV, accumulation/distribution) count as volume analysis, and at most 3 derived indicators (e.g. RSI, MACD, ADX) support the read;
	• never states a target without a stop;
	• checks every breakout and breakdown claim against volume;
	• labels counter-trend trades and speculative bottoming attempts as such;
	• states explicitly: "this setup fails if X," with X observable and dated where possible;
	• distinguishes observed price fact from pattern interpretation throughout.
Output structure
	1. Verdict: setup classification plus a one-line rationale, with as-of date and data window.
	2. Levels table: level / type / why it matters / what invalidates it.
	3. Evidence by workflow step: trend, momentum, volume, relative strength, positioning.
	4. Trade parameters when actionable: entry, stop, target, risk-reward, ATR-based sizing note.
	5. Update triggers and the forced-review date.
Write in English. Keep facts and interpretations visibly distinct. State uncertainty; a low-conviction read labeled as such is more useful than a confident guess.
