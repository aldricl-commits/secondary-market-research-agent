Analyze Stock Macro Regime
Objective
Convert current macro and market evidence into a falsifiable equity-risk view:
	1. Classify the Fed setup.
	2. Classify liquidity and detect deleveraging.
	3. Locate S&P 500 valuation within its history.
	4. Test the dominant market theme.
	5. Combine the layers into scenarios and relative risk-budget guidance.
Treat this as the macro overlay for stock analysis, not a substitute for company fundamentals.
Required reference
Read references/indicator-playbook.md before researching. Use its definitions, thresholds, scoring, and crisis override. Adapt thresholds when market structure changes; explain material deviations.
Evidence rules
	• Browse for every current analysis. Financial and macro conditions are time-sensitive.
	• State the analysis cutoff, market close used, and latest observation date for lagged releases.
	• Prefer primary sources: Federal Reserve, BLS, BEA, Treasury, New York Fed, FRED/source agency, company filings and investor relations.
	• Use reputable market-data or research sources only when primary data are unavailable.
	• Cite direct pages near the claims they support.
	• Separate observed facts, interpretation, and scenario assumptions.
	• Prefer direction and rate of change over isolated levels.
	• Use sequential measures such as 3-month annualized inflation alongside year-over-year readings.
	• Mark unavailable, stale, estimated, or conflicting data rather than silently filling gaps.
Workflow
1. Define scope
Identify:
	• target stock, index, portfolio, or market;
	• relevant currency and economic exposure;
	• investment horizon;
	• whether the user wants a current snapshot, historical comparison, monitoring checklist, or update.
If no security is specified, analyze the U.S. equity backdrop and S&P 500.
2. Classify the Fed setup
Research both sides of the dual mandate.
For labor, inspect unemployment, payroll momentum, revisions, participation, wage growth, claims, hires, quits, layoffs, and long-term unemployment. For inflation, inspect headline and core PCE, headline and core CPI, short-run annualized momentum, breadth, wages, shelter, services, goods, and energy or other supply shocks.
Classify the setup as one of:
	• hike;
	• hold with hike bias;
	• neutral hold;
	• hold with cut bias;
	• benign cut;
	• defensive or crisis cut.
Do not call all cuts bullish. A benign disinflation cut and a forced recession or liquidity cut have different equity implications.
Report the current state, likely next transition, evidence that would confirm it, and evidence that would falsify it.
3. Classify liquidity
Analyze three layers:
	1. Quantity: Fed assets and SOMA, reserve balances, TGA, ON RRP, Treasury issuance and tax-date flows.
	2. Price: SOFR minus IORB, EFFR minus IORB, repo-facility usage, dollar funding and cross-currency stress when relevant.
	3. Symptoms: credit spreads, volatility, Treasury-market functioning, market breadth, dollar, leveraged-asset behavior, and Bitcoin.
Check known event risks such as quarter-end, tax dates, Treasury cash rebuilding, central-bank changes, yen carry positioning, commodity shocks, and large forced liquidations.
Use Bitcoin only as a corroborating high-beta liquidity symptom. Account for crypto-specific ETF flows, regulation, stablecoin supply, leverage, and protocol events.
Classify liquidity as:
	• broad easing;
	• improving;
	• neutral or mixed;
	• tightening;
	• deleveraging or crisis.
4. Assess valuation
Use at least:
	• forward 12-month P/E against 5-, 10-, and preferably 20-year history;
	• CAPE or another cycle-smoothed measure;
	• an equity-versus-bond valuation measure such as forward earnings yield minus Treasury yield or a defensible implied equity risk premium;
	• earnings revisions and concentration context.
Classify valuation as cheap, fair, elevated, expensive, or extreme.
Treat valuation as an amplifier of outcomes and a constraint on future returns, not a short-term catalyst by itself.
5. Test the dominant theme
Identify the theme that explains the largest combination of:
	• capital expenditure;
	• earnings revisions;
	• index contribution;
	• market leadership;
	• investor attention.
Test it using:
	• end demand and backlog;
	• revenue and monetization;
	• margins, depreciation, free cash flow, and financing;
	• supply, inventories, and capacity;
	• breadth across beneficiaries;
	• price reaction to good and bad news.
Classify the theme as accelerating, intact, selective, increasingly debated, deteriorating, or broken. A stock falling on strong results is evidence that expectations may be outrunning fundamentals.
6. Map the macro regime to the target
For a specific stock, explain its sensitivity to:
	• discount rates and valuation duration;
	• cyclicality and labor or consumer demand;
	• funding needs and leverage;
	• dollar and commodity exposure;
	• liquidity beta;
	• dependence on the dominant theme.
Do not infer identical implications for every stock from the S&P 500 backdrop.
7. Combine states
Apply the crisis override before adding scores:
	• If liquidity or credit stress is confirmed, prioritize gross-exposure and leverage control regardless of rate-cut expectations or valuation.
	• Otherwise combine Fed, liquidity, valuation, and theme scores from the reference.
Express position implications relative to the investor's normal risk budget. Avoid unsupported personalized allocation prescriptions.
Build three to five mutually distinct scenarios. For each include:
	• state combination;
	• confirming triggers;
	• market mechanism;
	• likely winners and losers or target-stock implications;
	• risk-budget action;
	• falsification condition.
Use probabilities only when useful. Label them subjective, make them sum to 100%, and identify the next data that could change them.
Output structure
Lead with a one-paragraph conclusion, then provide:
	1. an as-of line and data-lag note;
	2. a compact state-and-score table;
	3. Fed setup;
	4. liquidity setup and crisis check;
	5. valuation percentile and earnings context;
	6. dominant-theme test;
	7. scenario matrix with actions and falsifiers;
	8. next catalysts and monitoring triggers.
Keep facts and inferences visibly distinct. Include uncertainty and avoid false precision.
Update mode
When updating a prior analysis:
	• preserve the previous framework and thresholds;
	• show what changed, what did not, and why the score moved;
	• distinguish data revisions from genuine economic change;
	• update scenarios only when evidence changes their probability or mechanism;
highlight newly triggered or cleared alerts.
